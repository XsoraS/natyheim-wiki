# Natyheim: Forgotten Past

Minecraft 26.1.2. Fabric and NeoForge. English and Turkish.

Seven civilisations lived here once. All seven fell, and nobody wrote down why.

Most archaeology mods hand you artefacts. This one wants you to figure out what killed the
people who buried them, and it will not tell you the answer. You dig, you look at what comes
up, and you decide what it means. If you're right, the Codex writes it down in your words.

Do it for all seven and you find out they died of the same thing.

## What you actually do

**Dig.** Suspicious sand and gravel, in seven kinds of site. Five are in the overworld, one
is in the Nether, one is out past the End islands.

**Read.** Relics carry Codex pages. Sherds are just broken clay until you put them back
together. Your journal opens straight from the inventory, so there's nothing to carry and
nothing to lose in a lava pit.

**Restore.** Three sherds and a lump of the right material on a restoration table. The
material decides whose pot you get back, which matters more than it sounds.

**Deduce.** Pin a relic, a sherd and one piece of physical evidence to an evidence board,
then commit to a reading. Get it right, get the page. Get it wrong, get your pins back.

**Open the sky.** Take a relic to a waygate ring after dark and light the runes in order.
Natyheim is up there: drifting islands, and seven sets of trials.

**Finish it.** Three tiers of arena per culture, then the Gate, then the Echo Sovereign.
Seven phases, one per civilisation. Each fragment you fused into the Ancient Core shuts one
of them up.

## The seven

| People | Where | What they left |
|---|---|---|
| Norse | taiga, plains, cold coasts | runestones, burial mounds, a sunken longship |
| Egyptian | desert, badlands | a small pyramid, a mummy chamber, a buried temple |
| Samurai | eastern forests, bamboo | an abandoned castle, a spirit shrine, bamboo ruins |
| Napoleonic | temperate Europe | fort ruins, a battlefield bunker, a headquarters |
| Mesopotamian | arid basins | a ziggurat, a tablet archive, a sandstorm gate |
| Ashkar | the Nether | a basalt hall with the forge still warm, a cinder quarry, the Ember Throne |
| Vael | the End | a spire with no stair, a walled chorus garden, the Last Geometry |

The Ashkar and the Vael aren't from this world. The mod doesn't pretend they are. They're
what the echo found when it got out.

## Nothing here is on a map

Five ways to find things that no `/locate` will give you.

**Surface finds.** Broken pot scattered on the ground, thicker the closer you get to
something buried. Walk with your eyes down. The sherds themselves are worth almost nothing;
the trail is the payment.

**Ancient roads.** Old paving, most of it gone, some of it still there. Roads go somewhere.
That's the whole appeal.

**The alignment.** Each people laid a chain of twelve survey stones along a single bearing.
Find two and you've got a line. Follow it far enough and there's a vault at the end that
nothing in the game will ever point you to. The stones get taller as you go the right way,
which is the only hint you get.

**Village rumours.** Ask a villager. Half of them know roughly where a findspot is and will
give you a direction and a rough distance. The other half know a story instead. Which one a
villager knows never changes, so there's no point asking twice.

**Cargo manifests.** Off wrecks. Each one lists goods and a destination the ship never
reached. Read it and you get a bearing to that people's nearest survey stone.

## A few things I'd point at

**You start with nothing.** No kit, no title card, no wall of text in chat. One toast in the
corner and then you're on your own. Handing someone a starting inventory decides for them
what they were going to go looking for.

**Chat stays clean.** Anything that repeats goes to the action bar. Sixteen advancements
still pop a toast and that's it.

**The evidence board** is the mechanic everything else is built around. Three finds and a
commitment.

**Charms are choices.** Twenty-one of them. The seven master charms have no effect list at
all: the Barrow Torc is brilliant in the dark and worthless at noon, the Forge Seal wants you
on fire, and the Pale Lodestone does nothing unless there's a long drop under your feet.

**Six metals, six signatures.** Not damage numbers. Bronze can't be slowed while you're
working. Silver makes undead flinch. Star-metal never lands hard.

**Puzzles differ per site.** Rune orders, chime sequences and echo-tile walks get shuffled
per structure, so the same ruin twice asks you two different questions.

**The Deeps are a warren.** The halls are laid out so you can walk everywhere without a
single door, but can't see across the room. The thing down there hunts by sound. That only
matters if there's something to hide behind.

**Building blocks on day one.** Seven masonry sets, stairs, slabs, walls, pillars, lamps, all
craftable from vanilla materials immediately. Nobody wants a build mod they have to earn.
