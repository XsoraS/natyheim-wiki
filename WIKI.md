## Home

Seven civilisations lived here once. All seven fell, and nobody wrote down why.

You're an archaeologist. You dig, you look at what comes up, and you decide what it means.
Nothing in this mod tells you the answer.

**Start here:** [Getting Started](#getting-started) · [Digging](#digging) ·
[The Codex](#the-codex-and-the-evidence-board)

**Reference:** [The Seven Peoples](#the-seven-peoples) · [Finding Sites](#finding-sites) ·

### What you're aiming at

Seven Codex deduction pages, one per people. Each one asks how that civilisation ended.
When you have all seven you'll notice they say the same thing, and that's when the mod opens
up: a sky dimension, seven sets of trials, and the Echo Sovereign at the end of them.

## Getting Started

You start with nothing. No kit, no quest log popping open, no text in chat. That's on
purpose.

**Press E.** There's a button at the top of your inventory screen. That's your journal: three
tabs, holding your quests, your Codex and your callings. A red dot on the button means
there's a reward waiting to be claimed.

**Get a brush.** Vanilla's copper brush works. The mod adds four better ones later.

**Find a dig site.** Wander. Look at the ground. See [Finding Sites](#finding-sites) — you
can also just travel and stumble into the structures, which are common enough.

**Brush suspicious sand and gravel.** Most brushes give you nothing or a piece of junk. That
is normal and it's meant to be. Roughly one brush in fourteen gives you a relic.

**Right-click a relic.** It writes a Codex page.

### Your first hour, honestly

Dig, get junk, get the occasional sherd, get one relic if you're lucky. Read the Codex page
it gives you. That page won't explain anything on its own. It's a fragment of a picture you
assemble later on the evidence board.

If you want a faster start, ask villagers. Crouch and right-click one empty-handed. Half of
them will point you at something.

## Digging

### Dig sites and strata

A dig site isn't one hole. It's a cut through time. Layers from top to bottom:

| Layer | What it is |
|---|---|
| Topsoil | Modern rubbish. Sticks, flint, bones. |
| Settlement | The people living ordinarily. |
| Collapse | Whatever happened to them. |
| Ruin | What fell on top afterwards. |
| Their Height | The good stuff, when they were at their strongest. |
| Founding | The first thing they built here. |
| Older Than All Seven | The dark band. Nobody put it there. |

Six suspicious blocks are seeded per site, one or more per band. Deeper is better, and the
dark band at the bottom is worth the descent.

The action bar tells you which layer you're in as you brush, along with your find count and
how far the next rank is.

### What comes out

Per brush, roughly:

- **39%** nothing at all
- **26%** period junk — flint, bone, brick, clay, coal
- **21%** a pottery sherd
- **7%** a relic
- **4%** something valuable — emerald, gold, amber, raw silver

A whole six-block site averages around 3.6 items and less than half a relic. You'll visit
two or three sites per relic. That's the intended rate.

### Ranks

Finds accumulate into a rank on the Dig Season track: Curious, Digger, Excavator, Surveyor,
Antiquarian, Master Archaeologist.

### Tools that help

- **Brushes** — four tiers above copper, each more durable.
- **The trowel** — reads the stratum you're standing in.
- **Survey pegs** — drive one in and the outline of the buried structure draws itself in the
  air, in that people's colour.
- **The sieve** — load it with spoil (gravel, sand, dirt) and it washes out what you missed.
- **Grid pegs and the dig tent** — a camp you can carry, with a respawn point and storage.

### Fragile finds

Some finds crack while you're working them. The action bar warns you. Stop brushing and let
it settle, or you'll get fragments instead of the whole thing.

## Finding Sites

Structures generate normally and `/locate` will find most of them. These five will not show
up on any map.

### Surface finds

Broken pot lying on the ground. One piece is litter. A dozen getting closer together means
something is buried nearby.

Walk with your eyes down. The scatter gets denser toward the middle, and the last ten blocks
change character sharply. Dig at the thickest point and you'll hit a deposit — five
suspicious blocks with the best archaeology loot in the game, because nothing led you there
but your own eyes.

The sherds themselves are almost worthless. Don't farm them; read them.

### Ancient roads

Fragments of old paving, running in lines across the world. Most of it is gone. Follow what's
left and it goes somewhere.

### The alignment

This is the one puzzle the game never explains.

Each of the seven peoples laid a chain of twelve survey stones along a single bearing. A
stone is a small monument: a stone-brick plinth, a polished shaft, and a coloured cap in that
people's colour.

Find two stones of the same colour, note the coordinates, and you have a line.

The stones get **taller** as the chain goes on. Two courses at the start, six at the end.
That's how you know which way to walk. Follow it past the last stone and there's a buried
vault holding that people's fragment and the best chest in the mod.

Nothing will ever point you at it.

| People | Cap colour |
|---|---|
| Norse | light blue |
| Egyptian | yellow |
| Samurai | red |
| Napoleonic | blue |
| Mesopotamian | brown |
| Ashkar | orange |
| Vael | magenta |

### Village rumours

Crouch and right-click a villager with an empty hand.

Half of them give you a direction and a rough distance to the nearest surface-find site.
The other half name something missing from your Codex instead. Which one a villager knows
never changes, so there's no point asking the same person twice — walk to the next village.

### Cargo manifests

Found on wrecks. Each lists cargo and a destination the ship never reached. Right-click one
and it gives you the bearing to that people's nearest survey stone.

If you never noticed the alignment on your own, this is how you find it.

## The Codex and the Evidence Board

### Codex pages

Relics write pages. So do pots you restore, places you visit, and things you kill. Sixty-six
pages in total. They're stored on your character, not in an item, so you can't lose them.

Most pages are observations. They don't add up on their own.

### Restoration

Three sherds plus one lump of a people's own material, on a **restoration table**, gives you
that people's pot back. The material decides whose pot it is, not the sherds.

Restoration comes out at a grade. Better grades look better and are worth more.

### Conservation

Relics come out of the ground **corroded**. A corroded relic reads for less and sells for
less. The **conservation bench** cleans them. The tooltip on a corroded relic tells you this.

### Dating

The **dating bench** tells you which people a sherd or relic belongs to when it isn't
obvious.

### Forgeries

Not everything you buy is real. A forged relic looks right and reads wrong. The analysis lens
and the dating bench are how you tell.

### The evidence board

The heart of the mod.

Pin three things: a **relic**, a **sherd**, and one piece of **physical evidence** — amber
from archive seals, a blaze rod that was cut rather than dropped, powder from a magazine,
chorus fruit from a tended row. Then commit to a reading of what happened to those people.

Right, and you get the deduction page. Wrong, and you get your pins back and nothing else.

Seven boards, seven answers. The seventh one is the point of the mod.

## The Seven Peoples

| People | Where they are | What they left |
|---|---|---|
| **Norse** | taiga, plains, cold coasts | runestones, burial mounds, a sunken longship |
| **Egyptian** | desert, badlands | a small pyramid, a mummy chamber, a buried temple |
| **Samurai** | eastern forests, bamboo | an abandoned castle, a spirit shrine, bamboo ruins |
| **Napoleonic** | temperate Europe | fort ruins, a battlefield bunker, a headquarters |
| **Mesopotamian** | arid basins | a ziggurat, a tablet archive, a sandstorm gate |
| **Ashkar** | **the Nether** | a basalt hall, a cinder quarry, the Ember Throne |
| **Vael** | **the End** | a spire with no stair, a walled chorus garden, the Last Geometry |

The Ashkar are forge-kings out of the burning underworld who cut their halls from basalt and
kept their dead in ash. The Vael built at the world's edge and left geometry, silence, and
nothing that says what they wanted.

Neither is from this world. They're what the echo found when it got out.

### Masters

Each people has a master — a boss guarding their fragment. Seven masters, seven fragments.
Each master has a second phase.

## Charms

Twenty-one charms, three per people. They go in dedicated slots in your inventory, not your
armour slots.

The seven **master charms** have no effect list at all. They're conditional, and the
condition is the whole design:

- **Barrow Torc** (Norse) — the strongest thing in the early game *in the dark*. Jewellery at
  noon.
- **Forge Seal** (Ashkar) — wants you on fire.
- **Pale Lodestone** (Vael) — does nothing unless there's an eight-block drop under your feet.

The rest follow the same rule. A charm that's always on isn't a decision.

## Metals and Armour

Six metals. Each carries a **signature** — a rule about the world rather than a number on a
bar.

| Metal | Signature |
|---|---|
| Bronze | can't be slowed while you're working |
| Silver | makes the undead flinch |
| Orichalcum | — |
| Tamahagane | — |
| Star-metal | never lands hard |

Silver has a full tool and armour set of its own.

## The Deeps

Under the world, older than any of the seven.

The halls are laid out as a warren. You can walk everywhere without opening a single door,
but you can't see across a room from any corner. The perimeter runs low and tight; the middle
opens up. There are alcoves cut into the walls to stand still in.

That layout exists because of what hunts down there. It hunts by **sound**. Crouch and it
never knows you were there — and that only means something if there's somewhere to be unseen.

Light hangs only where a wall ends, so corners are decisions.

Nine dungeon types, plus the broken stair — a ceremonial stairwell that used to go much
further down than it does now.

## Natyheim and the Trials

### Opening the sky

Take a relic to a waygate ring **after dark** and light its runes in the right order.
Natyheim opens: a sky dimension of drifting islands.

### The trials

Three tiers of arena per culture, twenty-one in total. Each arena is a sealed bedrock pocket
with an **Arena Core** at the centre that spawns waves and watches them die. Clear the last
wave and the vault seal opens.

The field isn't flat. There are terraces on two flanks with steps on the inward faces only,
so high ground is somewhere you can hold and somewhere you can be cornered.

Tier 3's vault holds that culture's fragment.

### The Gate and the Sovereign

Fuse all seven fragments into the **Ancient Core**, then the Gate answers.

The **Echo Sovereign** has seven phases, one per civilisation. Every fragment you fused
silences one of them.

## FAQ

**Where's the Codex item?**
There isn't one. Press E and use the button at the top of your inventory.

**Why did I get nothing from brushing?**
Because most brushes give nothing. That's the intended rate. Keep going and go deeper.

**My relic says "corroded".**
Clean it at a conservation bench. It reads and sells for less until you do.

**A red dot appeared on my journal button.**
A quest reward is ready. Open the journal and click the item you want — if a quest offers
several, you pick.

**Structures spawning underwater or floating?**
They shouldn't. If you find one, that's a bug worth reporting with coordinates.

**Can I play this on a server?**
Yes. There's no custom networking, so it behaves the same in single player and multiplayer.
Two blocks (the pair lever, and one door type) actually need a second player.

**Does it work with other mods?**
It adds features to overworld biomes and puts loot in its own structures only. It doesn't
overwrite any vanilla loot table.

**Fabric or NeoForge?**
Both, from the same feature set.
